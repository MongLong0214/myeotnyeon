# Antonio Brown (ex-NFL star, Miami shooting) — 3 years probation

Miami-Dade Circuit Court (Florida state court), Judge Marisa Tinkler Mendez. First instance. Plea and sentence on the same day, 2026-11-06 (Friday). Original charge: attempted second-degree murder, which carried up to 30 years with a 20-year mandatory minimum. He pleaded guilty to a reduced charge, aggravated assault with a firearm (Miami Herald says "with a deadly weapon"), under a plea deal. No trial.

- F1: On May 16, 2025, outside a celebrity boxing match in Miami, Brown grabbed a handgun from a security staffer and fired two shots at a man he had been in a fistfight with earlier. The man said one bullet grazed his neck. (NBC News, ESPN, ABC7/AP)
- F2: Brown said he was defending himself and claimed others were trying to steal his jewelry. (CBS Miami/AP)
- F3: He was extradited from Dubai in November 2025 after a June 2025 arrest warrant. (CBS Miami/AP, ABC7)
- F4: The man who was shot told prosecutors he would no longer take part in the case. The deal came about two weeks after that. (ESPN, NBC News)
- F5: Prosecutor Howard Rosen said there was "a question" about whether Brown meant to hit the man, who was walking away at the time. (ESPN)
- F6: In court Brown spoke quietly and the judge asked him to speak up. Outside, he thanked God and said he wanted to "get back to life" and his family. (ESPN, CBS Miami/AP)
- F7: Defense attorney Mark Eiglarsh: "My client was facing 30 years in prison with a 20-year minimum mandatory, and he was given an offer he just couldn't refuse." (CBS Miami/AP, NBC News)

## Exact sentence

- **3 years of probation. No jail or prison time.** (AP via CBS Miami/ABC News, ESPN, NBC News, Miami Herald)
- Conditions: weekly counseling sessions and a ban on possessing a firearm, "among other conditions" (AP/ESPN). NBC adds a mandatory psychological evaluation.
- **No criminal conviction under the plea.** He can ask to end probation early at about the halfway point if he meets all terms. (ABC News/AP; ESPN: "no criminal conviction")
- None of the fetched sources mention restitution, a fine, or costs, so do not state any.

## Asks

- This was a negotiated plea, so there was no contested sentencing. Prosecutors offered the reduced charge with probation. Rosen's reason was doubt about whether Brown intended to hit the man (ESPN).
- Defense took the offer and called it one "he just couldn't refuse" (AP).
- The real contrast is what he faced: the attempted-murder charge carried up to 30 years with a 20-year mandatory minimum (NBC, ESPN, AP).

## Quiz options

- A) 3 years of probation, no jail — **correct**
- B) 20 years in prison (the mandatory minimum he faced)
- C) 18 months in jail, then probation (plausible-sounding middle option)

## What NOT to say

- Do not say he was "convicted" or is a "convicted felon". The plea carries no criminal conviction (AP).
- Do not say "attempted murder" except as the original charge. He pleaded to aggravated assault with a firearm.
- Do not name or show the man who was shot. Say "a man he had fought earlier".
- Do not say "he shot a man" as proven fact. Say "fired two shots" and "the man said a bullet grazed his neck".
- Do not say the victim "forgave him" or "dropped the charges". Only prosecutors can drop charges. Safe wording: "the man stopped cooperating".
- Do not mention fines or restitution, and no domestic-violence or child-support history (that would bring in other parties).
- Not political, not a sex crime, no death, no children, not Korean-linked.

## Appeal status

Negotiated guilty plea with no reported appeal (AP, ESPN, NBC). Pleas are rarely appealed. Say "was sentenced", not "case closed forever", because probation can be violated.
